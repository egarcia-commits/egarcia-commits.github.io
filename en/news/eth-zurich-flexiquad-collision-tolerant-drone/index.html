ETH Zurich's FlexiQuad: a soft-frame drone that keeps flying after collisions

Researchers built a quadrotor with a deformable frame that absorbs impacts yet reaches 80 km/h, opening the door to inspection and rescue flights in tight, cluttered spaces.

#research#fpv#inspection

Researchers at ETH Zurich have developed FlexiQuad, a four-rotor drone whose frame is designed to deform in a controlled way when it hits an obstacle, instead of breaking. A patent application has been filed for the structural design.

  • The frame stays stiff exactly where forces must be transmitted to keep flying, and flexes everywhere else.
  • Despite being "soft", FlexiQuad reaches speeds of up to 80 km/h with accelerations comparable to rigid drones.
  • In tests it was flown into obstacles at around 25 km/h and continued flying after the impact.
  • The flexible structure can also be used to grasp and carry objects.

Target applications include inspecting ship ballast tanks, search and rescue in collapsed buildings, and environmental measurements in dense forests. The team even suggests fitting a net in the hollow centre to capture rogue drones.

Why it matters: collision tolerance is one of the biggest barriers to flying close to structures. Combined with low-latency HD video, it could make confined-space FPV inspection far safer and cheaper.
